## Step 5 — Fix what failed

For anything that failed, fix the **description**, not the instructions — the instructions never get read if the description doesn't get picked in the first place.

**If a "should trigger" phrasing failed:** your "when" sentence is too narrow. Widen it to cover the real variation you just found.

**If a "should not trigger" phrasing wrongly passed:** your "when" sentence is too broad. Either narrow it, or add an explicit exclusion sentence: `Does NOT cover [the specific nearby thing].`

---

## When to add an explicit exclusion

Add a `Does NOT cover...` sentence when:

- The skill's topic sits near other, different tasks that share vocabulary (commit vs. revert, deploy vs. rollback).
- Step 3 above actually turned up a real near-miss — don't add exclusions preemptively for things that never came up.

Skip it when the skill is already narrow and distinctive enough that nothing realistically overlaps.

---

## The final read-through

Before you consider the description done, read it one more time as if you'd never seen this skill before. Ask: **could someone who has never met me or read the instructions tell, from this one line alone, exactly when to use this and when not to?**

If the honest answer is no, go back to Step 1.
