## What each case study contains

Every case study follows the same shape, on purpose — so once you know how to read one, you can read all four quickly:

1. **The problem** — what this person was doing by hand, before the skill.
2. **The thought process** — how they decided a skill was the right tool (Chapter 6's checklist, applied for real).
3. **The real `SKILL.md`** — copy-paste ready.
4. **What went wrong the first time** — every one of these skills failed on its first draft, exactly like yours did in Chapter 3. That's included on purpose, not smoothed over.
5. **How it was tested** before anyone else used it.
6. **Where it sits on the sharing ladder** from Chapter 8, and why.
7. **References & assets** — the real, standalone `SKILL.md` (plus any bundled scripts or policy files), a case-study-specific diagram in its own `assets/` folder, and links to the same team member's counterpart in AI-Workflows and AI-Agents.

---

## Why these four, specifically

Frontend, backend, QA, and code review cover four genuinely different shapes of problem:

- **Frontend (Divya)** — a skill that checks something *subjective enough to need real judgement*, not just a fixed rule.
- **Backend (Vikram)** — a skill that *generates real code*, following a structural convention.
- **QA (Ananya)** — a skill where a **false positive is worse than a false negative** — a big shift from your commit-message skill, where it was the other way round.
- **Code review (Rahul)** — a skill that has to stay **consistent across an entire team**, not just useful to one person.

Seeing the same nine-chapter process bend to fit four different shapes of problem is the actual point of reading all four — not just the four finished skills themselves.
