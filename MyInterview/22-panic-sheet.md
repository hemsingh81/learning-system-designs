## Five phrases that land

1. *"Never theory alone — I attach every point to a project, a decision, and a number."*
2. *"I design top-down and I still build bottom-up — nineteen years of code is why my architecture survives contact with reality."*
3. *"I land data into an operational store and a separate analytical store, so a heavy historical query can never slow the report with the morning deadline."*
4. *"For a deadline-driven system, support is a clock, not a queue."*
5. *"AI speeds up the engineer, it doesn't replace them — the human is accountable for every line."*

---

## The two frameworks (so no answer rambles)

- **STAR-D** (experience): **S**ituation → **T**ask → **A**ction → **R**esult → **D** = the lesson.
- **C-QUAD** (design): **C**larify → **Q**ualities (NFRs) → **U**nknowns → **A**rchitecture (one diagram) → **D**ecisions (chosen vs rejected + trade-off).

---

## The reset (if a question floors me)

1. **Pause — it is allowed.** Two seconds of silence beats filler.
2. **Restate the question** in my own words — buys time, confirms I understood.
3. **Start from a project.** "This reminds me of when I…" — a story is always easier than a definition.
4. **If I truly don't know:** say how I'd find out. *"I haven't used that exact tool, but here's how I'd approach it…"* — honest beats bluff, every time.

---

## Three things to do in the room

- **Land a number in the first two answers** — sets the tone that I speak in evidence.
- **Ask one sharp question back** — shows I think like an owner, not a candidate.
- **Slow down.** A 90-second answer with a number beats a four-minute ramble every time.
