# 24 · Role-Tailoring Guide (adapt the kit to any job description)

[← Offer & Salary Negotiation](23-offer-negotiation.md) · [Home](README.md) · [Next → STAR Story Bank](25-star-story-bank.md)

No two roles are the same, so I never walk in with a generic pitch. Twenty minutes tailoring the kit to *this* job description is the highest-value prep I do. This page is the checklist for that: how to read a JD, which files and stories to emphasise, and how to lead with the version of me the role actually wants.

> **My rule for tailoring:** *same facts, different emphasis.* I never invent experience — I re-order the truth so the most relevant proof comes first.

## The 20-minute tailoring routine

1. **(5 min) Read the JD** and highlight the top 5 things it repeats or leads with.
2. **(5 min) Map** each of those to an anchor project (A–E) and the kit files that cover it.
3. **(5 min) Pick** the pitch version and the *one* lead story I'll open with.
4. **(5 min) Spot gaps** — anything the JD wants that I'm light on — and prepare an honest bridge.

That's it. Four steps, and I walk in sounding like I was built for the role — because I've surfaced the parts of my real experience that fit it.

---

## Step 1 — read the JD like an architect

I read a job description the way I read a requirement: what is it *really* optimising for? I look for:

- **What's repeated.** If "data platform" or "AI" or "stakeholder management" appears five times, that's the centre of gravity.
- **What's first.** The order of responsibilities usually reflects priority.
- **The verbs.** "Own", "build", "lead", "advise" tell me how hands-on vs advisory the role is.
- **The must-haves vs nice-to-haves.** I make sure every must-have maps to a real story.
- **The hidden pain.** A JD often hints at the problem they're hiring to fix ("scaling challenges", "legacy migration", "first architect"). That pain is my opening.

## Step 3 — pick the right pitch and lead story

I have four pitch versions in [10 Pitch & Resume](10-pitch-and-resume.md). I choose deliberately:

| Role type | Pitch to lead with |
|---|---|
| Architecture / senior technical | [2-minute pitch](10-pitch-and-resume.md#the-2-minute-pitch) |
| Deep-technical / coding-heavy | [hands-on version](10-pitch-and-resume.md#the-hands-on-version) |
| Client panel / pursuit / consulting | [pre-sales version](10-pitch-and-resume.md#the-pre-sales-version) |
| Quick screen / networking | [30-second pitch](10-pitch-and-resume.md#the-30-second-pitch) |

Then I pick **one lead story** — the anchor project closest to the JD's centre of gravity — and rehearse it cold. First impression is the anchor; I make it the most relevant one.

## Quick role profiles

Four common shapes and exactly how I'd tune the same experience for each.

**▸ Data-Platform Architect.** Lead with A (Aladdin → SQL Server & Snowflake, daily deadline). Emphasise the operational/analytical split, orchestration, validation/retry/reconciliation, and the numbers from D. Expect deep SQL and pipeline questions — [17](17-deepdive-python-data.md) + [19](19-performance-deep-dive.md).

**▸ AI / ML Solutions Architect.** Lead with B (reference architecture + first production RAG). Emphasise the four pillars, grounding, evaluation, governance in a regulated firm, and how I'd roll AI out to a team — [20](20-ai-assisted-development.md). Be ready for "RAG vs fine-tuning" and anti-hallucination.

**▸ Hands-on / Lead Engineer-Architect.** Lead with the [hands-on pitch](10-pitch-and-resume.md#the-hands-on-version) and A's build details (I wrote the FastAPI ETL, the reusable Web API pattern, the React screens). Expect a live coding round — [14](14-fullstack-hands-on.md) + [18](18-coding-round-prep.md). Have [21](21-objections-and-tough-questions.md) ready for "are you still hands-on enough".

**▸ Enterprise / Consulting Architect.** Lead with the [pre-sales pitch](10-pitch-and-resume.md#the-pre-sales-version) and C/E (stakeholders, on-site regulated delivery, pursuits). Emphasise owning commitments end to end and running production well — [05](05-client-engagement.md), [06](06-rfp-presales.md), [07](07-support-post-delivery.md).

## Step 4 — check for gaps and red flags

**My own gaps.** If the JD wants something I'm genuinely light on (a specific cloud, a niche tool), I don't hide it — I prepare an honest bridge: the closest thing I *have* done and how fast I'd close the gap. My [objections file](21-objections-and-tough-questions.md) has the ready phrasing for "only Microsoft", "jack of all trades", etc.

**Their red flags.** Tailoring runs both ways — I read the JD for warning signs too: no mention of production ownership (do they hand off and vanish?), vague scope, "wear many hats" as code for understaffed, or a title mismatch. I turn these into questions I ask them.

**Questions I ask back** (tuned to the role): *"What does success look like in the first 90 days?"*, *"Who owns production when something breaks?"*, *"How hands-on is this architect expected to be?"* — the answers tell me which version of me they actually need, and whether the role is real.
