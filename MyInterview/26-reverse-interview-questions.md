# 26 · Reverse-Interview Questions (the ones I ask them)

[← STAR Story Bank](25-star-story-bank.md) · [Home](README.md) · [Next → My First 90 Days](27-first-90-days.md)

The best question I get asked is *"do you have any questions for us?"* — because a sharp question makes me sound like an owner, not a candidate. I go in with questions grouped by *who* is in front of me, because a hiring manager, a peer architect and a CTO each reveal different truths. I always keep 2–3 in reserve and I take the answers seriously — I'm interviewing them too.

> **My rule for reverse questions:** *ask what a person who already owns the role would need to know.* Never ask what a 30-second search would answer.

## For the hiring manager

This person owns whether I succeed day to day. I ask about success, scope and reality.

- **"What does success look like for this role in the first 90 days, and then in the first year?"** — tells me if they have a real plan or just a vacancy.
- **"How hands-on do you actually want this architect to be — designing, or designing *and* building?"** — confirms the role matches my hands-on positioning.
- **"Who owns production when something breaks at 3 a.m. — the team that built it, or a separate ops group?"** — reveals whether they value end-to-end ownership like I do.
- **"What's the hardest technical problem the team is facing right now?"** — surfaces the real pain, and lets me show how I'd approach it.
- **"What would make you say, a year from now, that hiring me was clearly the right call?"** — gets the honest, unwritten expectations.

---

## For a peer architect or engineer

This is where I learn the *truth* about how the place really works. Engineers tell you what managers won't.

- **"Walk me through what happens from a developer committing code to it running in production — how automated is it?"** — tests real CI/CD maturity vs slideware.
- **"How are architecture decisions actually made and recorded here — is there an ADR habit, or is it tribal knowledge?"** — shows if design is disciplined or ad hoc.
- **"What's the biggest piece of technical debt you'd fix if you had a free sprint?"** — honest signal of the codebase's health.
- **"How do you use AI-assisted development day to day, and who's accountable for what the AI writes?"** — lets me connect to my [AI-in-team playbook](20-ai-assisted-development.md).
- **"When you disagree with an architectural decision, what happens?"** — tells me if it's a safe, evidence-driven culture like the one I try to build.

---

## For the CTO or senior leader

Here I ask about direction, bets and how technology connects to the business.

- **"Where do you want the platform / architecture to be in two years, and what's the biggest thing standing in the way?"** — shows if there's a real strategy I'd be helping deliver.
- **"How does the engineering org decide what to build — how close is it to the business and its customers?"** — reveals whether architects get a seat at the table.
- **"What's your stance on build-vs-buy and on adopting AI in regulated workflows?"** — lets me show my governance-first thinking from Project B.
- **"What does technical leadership look like above the architect level here — what's the growth path?"** — signals whether this is a step or a ceiling.

---

## For HR / recruiter

Process, team and logistics — asked warmly.

- **"What are the next steps and the timeline from here?"** — practical and expected.
- **"Why is this role open — is it new growth or a backfill?"** — tells me a lot about stability and expectations.
- **"How would you describe the team's culture in one honest sentence?"** — an easy, revealing question.

---

## My top 5 (if I only get a minute)

When time is short, these five earn the most signal:

1. *"What does success look like in the first 90 days?"*
2. *"How hands-on do you want this architect to be — design, or design and build?"*
3. *"Who owns production when it breaks?"*
4. *"What's the hardest technical problem the team faces right now?"*
5. *"What's the biggest tech debt you'd fix with a free sprint?"*

---

## What their answers tell me

I'm interviewing them too, so I listen for the signal behind the answer.

| If they say… | I read it as… |
|---|---|
| A clear 90-day plan | Mature team, real role, good sign |
| "We'll figure it out when you start" | Vague scope — dig deeper before accepting |
| "A separate ops team owns production" | Design-and-hand-off culture — may clash with my end-to-end style |
| "Decisions are mostly tribal" | Low architecture discipline — an opportunity, or a warning |
| Honest about a hard problem / real tech debt | Trustworthy, self-aware team |
| Can't name any weakness | Guarded culture — note it |

---

## Questions I never ask

- Anything answered by a 30-second look at their website or the JD.
- Salary/benefits in an *early technical* round — I save that for the right stage (see [Offer & Salary Negotiation](23-offer-negotiation.md)).
- "What does the company do?" — shows I didn't prepare.
- Only logistics and none about the work — makes me look uninterested in the role itself.
